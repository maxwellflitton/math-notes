The product rule can be denoted with the following:
$$
\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)g(x)] = f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x)
$$

## Connections
- Part of [[Calculus]]; sits alongside [[chain-rule|the chain rule]] (for composites) and the first-principles definition in [[lim-zero-example]].
- Applied and tidied: differentiating the Bernstein terms $t^i(1-t)^{n-i}$ of [[Bezier Curves]] is what turns a cubic's velocity into a quadratic whose control points are the differences $P_{i+1} - P_i$.
- Run **backwards** it becomes [[by-parts|integration by parts]] — that note derives the formula by integrating both sides of the rule above.
- It is the step that collapses the left-hand side when solving [[Linear Differential Equations]] with an integrating factor.
- Used to verify antiderivatives by differentiating back — see the checks in [[by-parts]] and [[exponents]].
